## 2. Amazon drought and fire

- Jiménez-Muñoz et al. 2016, *Sci. Rep.* 6, 33130 (DOI 10.1038/srep33130, full text read): Oct 2015 LST 1.5 °C above Oct 1997 peak; 13% of rainforest in extreme drought Feb–Mar 2016. HIGH.
- Aragão et al. 2018, *Nat. Commun.* 9, 536 (DOI 10.1038/s41467-017-02771-y, full text): fire incidence +36% in 2015 vs prior 12-yr mean; drought-year forest-fire CO₂ 989±504 Tg/yr vs 454±496 average. HIGH.
- Correction: Phillips et al. 2009 *Science* (DOI 10.1126/science.1164033) is the 2005 ATLANTIC drought — not El Niño; do not cite as such. For 2015-16 mortality use Leitold et al. 2018, *New Phytologist* 219, 959–971 (DOI 10.1111/nph.15110).
- 2023-24: Rio Negro at Manaus 12.70 m on 26 Oct 2023, lowest since 1902 — Espinoza et al. 2024, *Sci. Rep.* 14, 8107 (DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-58782-5, full text via PMC); Oct 2023 temp anomaly +3 °C (1981–2010 baseline); SW Amazon rainfall −41 mm/month, comparable to 1997-98's −42. HIGH.
- WWA Amazon attribution (24 Jan 2024, fetched primary, verbatim): "El Niño reduced the amount of precipitation in the region by about the same amount as climate change; however, the strong drying trend was almost entirely due to increased global temperatures, so the severity of the drought currently being experienced is largely driven by climate change." Meteorological drought 10× more likely; agricultural (SPEI) ~30×. HIGH.
- Lake Tefé dolphins (>200 deaths, 41 °C water): secondary only, MEDIUM.

## 3. Peru/Ecuador coastal flooding

- Mechanism (Kelvin waves, coastal-El Niño taxonomy): Peng et al. 2019 *Nat. Commun.* 10, 298; Echevin et al. 2018 *Front. Mar. Sci.* (DOI 10.3389/fmars.2018.00367); Hu et al. 2019 *Clim. Dyn.* 52, 7537–7552. HIGH.
- 1997-98: WHO Disease Outbreak News 25 Feb 1998 (primary): 2,863 cholera cases Jan 1998 vs 174 a year earlier, attributed to El Niño floods. Deaths/cost: report as range (200–340 deaths, ~$2B), MEDIUM-LOW.
- Correction: "Takahashi & Martínez 2017 (Clim. Dyn.)" is about the 1925 event. For 2017: Takahashi et al. 2018 *BAMS* 99, S210–S211; Garreaud 2018 *Int. J. Climatol.* 38(S1), e1296–e1302 (DOI 10.1002/joc.5426); Rodríguez-Morata et al. 2019 *Clim. Dyn.* 52, 5605–5622; Ramírez & Briones 2017 *IJDRS* 8, 489–492 (~$3.1B damage, 1.1M affected); Yglesias-González et al. 2023 *PLOS ONE* (DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0290767, full text): 169 deaths, 194,000 displaced, ~$7.7B reconstruction, 68,290 dengue cases. Damage range $1.4–7.7B by accounting scope — report as range.
- 2023 Cyclone Yaku: first cyclone there in ~40 yrs, ~85,000 homes damaged (MEDIUM-HIGH, secondary). 2023 Peru dengue: ~200,000–270,000 cases, 200–440 deaths across sources (MEDIUM).
- **2023-24 coastal "bust" (VERIFIED, two Peruvian government primary sources):** IGP ICEN peaked only "weak" (+0.98 °C Feb 2024) vs late-2023 forecast of 77% moderate/strong; SENAMHI documented north-coast rainfall deficits up to −100% Dec 2023–Mar 2024, after ~S/4.5bn (~$1.2B) preparedness allocation. MEDIUM-HIGH. (Unresolved: CIIFEN "most intense coastal El Niño in 20 years" claim — page unreachable, likely duration-based; not asserted.)

## 4. Southeastern South America wet signal (DJF)

- Mechanism (PSA wave train, subtropical jet, LLJ moisture): Grimm & Tedeschi 2009 *J. Climate* 22, 1589–1609 (DOI 10.1175/2008jcli2429.1); Tedeschi/Cavalcanti/Grimm series in *Int. J. Climatol.* (DOIs 10.1002/joc.4106, 10.1002/joc.3519, 10.1002/joc.4670); Barros, Doyle & Camilloni 2008 *Theor. Appl. Climatol.* 93, 19–33; Cazes Boezio et al. 2012 *Rev. Bras. Meteor.* 27, 377–387. Citations HIGH.
- 2015-16: Uruguay/Paraná/Paraguay floods late Dec 2015, 100,000–150,000+ evacuated, "worst in 50 years" (consistent secondary; MEDIUM).
- May 2024 Rio Grande do Sul floods (~600,000 displaced, >180 dead): OUTSIDE the canonical DJF window, decay phase. Clarke et al., *npj Natural Hazards* 3, art. 1 (DOI 10.1038/s44304-025-00162-8, Crossref abstract verbatim): warming made the event "approximately 2 (0.06–4200) times as likely"; "the recent El Niño event also approximately doubled (0.7–37 times) the likelihood." Comparable multipliers, enormous uncertainty ranges — flag when citing. HIGH on numbers.

## 5. Central America / Caribbean drought

- Canícula/midsummer drought: Magaña, Amador & Medina 1999 *J. Climate* 12, 1577–1588; Karnauskas et al. 2013 *Atmósfera* 26, 261–281; Maldonado et al. 2016 *Adv. Geosci.* 42, 35–50 (ENSO connection); Anderson et al. 2019 *ERL* 14, 124016. Citations HIGH.
- 2015-16: FAO/FEWS NET/ACAPS — >3.5M food insecure across Central America; Dry Corridor ~500k severe + ~1.3M moderate; "worst drought in decades" (agency sources, MEDIUM-HIGH).
- 2023-24 Panama Canal: Gatún Lake 85+ ft → 79.6 ft; transits 36–38 → 22–24/day; auction slots to $4–5M (well-corroborated secondary, MEDIUM — ACP primary page unreachable).
- **WWA Panama attribution (1 May 2024, fetched primary): "we cannot conclude that the observed drying is attributable to human-caused climate change"; ~5%/yr likelihood under El Niño; water demand growth a compounding factor. Opposite conclusion from the Amazon WWA study — a useful paired contrast for the Comment.** HIGH.

## 6. North America DJF

- Canonical: Ropelewski & Halpert 1986 *MWR* 114, 2352–2362 (N. America-specific); R&H 1987, 1989; Halpert & Ropelewski 1992 *J. Climate* 5, 577–593. CPC's operational impacts page cites exactly these (fetched). HIGH: wet Gulf/SE, dry-warm PNW/Ohio Valley, deepened Aleutian Low, warm northern tier.
- **2015-16 California bust (5 corroborating papers, all Crossref-confirmed):** Siler et al. 2017 *J. Climate* 30, 10067–10079 (Indo-west Pacific SSTs overrode ENSO forcing); Zhang et al. 2018 *J. Climate* 31, 555–574 ("the Failed 2016 Winter Rains"); Jong et al. 2016 *ERL* 11, 054021 (nonlinear, SoCal-focused relationship); L'Heureux et al. 2017 *BAMS* 98, 1363–1382. HIGH.
- 2023-24: record Feb 2024 LA storms (7.03 in over Feb 4–5, breaking 1913 record; 4th-wettest Feb; >300 landslides) — consistent news, MEDIUM-HIGH. DJF 2023-24 warmest winter on record for contiguous US; southern wet signal "largely verified" but exact NCEI percentile rankings UNVERIFIED.
- El Niño → reduced US winter heating demand: NOT FOUND in citable primary source this session; UNVERIFIED.

## Signal failures / surprises summary (for the piece's honesty layer)

1. 2015-16 Southern California: strong event, near-normal rain (best-documented failure).
2. 2023-24 Peru coastal: $1.2B prepositioned for floods that never came (ICEN peaked weak; deficits to −100%). Pairs with Peru's 2023 execution problem in policy-evidence.md: prepare for the *distribution*, not the deterministic forecast.
3. 2023-24 Amazon: WWA says warming, not El Niño, drove drought severity.
4. 2023-24 Panama Canal: WWA says El Niño (not warming) was primary — opposite of Amazon.
5. May 2024 Rio Grande do Sul: outside canonical season; warming and El Niño roughly equal multipliers with huge error bars.
