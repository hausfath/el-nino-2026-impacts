## IPCC AR6 WG1 (verified by direct PDF download + text extraction)

- **Annex IV AIV.2.3.2**, verbatim: "Amongst the best-known teleconnections are the association of El Niño events with increased precipitation in the south-western and south-eastern USA, eastern Africa, coastal Ecuador and Peru, and northern Argentina, and with decreased precipitation in eastern Australia and the Maritime Continent, southern Africa, the Amazonian basin and much of the Indian subcontinent."
- **Ch 3 §3.7.3**: "low confidence in the role of a human-induced influence" in observed ENSO amplitude changes; "no clear evidence for a recent sustained shift in ENSO beyond the range of variability."
- **Ch 4 ES/§4.5.3**, verbatim: "It is virtually certain that ENSO will remain the dominant mode of interannual variability in a warmer world. There is no model consensus for a systematic change in intensity of ENSO SST variability over the 21st century… However, it is very likely that ENSO rainfall variability… will increase significantly." Also: "It is likely that the pattern of ENSO teleconnection over the North Pacific and North America will shift eastward."
- **Key discipline for the piece: the very-likely increase in ENSO rainfall variability is a projection, not a detection. Do not conflate.**

## Indonesia / Maritime Continent

- Page et al. 2002, *Nature* 420, 61–65. DOI 10.1038/nature01131 (fetched full text). 1997 Indonesian fires: 0.81–2.57 Gt C released, equal to 13–40% of mean annual global fossil-fuel emissions; largest annual CO₂ growth-rate anomaly since 1957.
- Field et al. 2016, *PNAS* 113, 9204–9209. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1524888113. 2015 most severe fire season of the satellite era but "ranked after 1997 and alongside 1991 and 1994" in longer visibility records; nonlinear fire response below ~4 mm/day rainfall on degraded peat. (The widely quoted "1.6–1.75 Gt CO₂-eq for 2015" is from secondary journalism — UNVERIFIED against GFED/primary.)
- Koplitz et al. 2016, *ERL* 11, 094023. DOI 10.1088/1748-9326/11/9/094023 (abstract fetched): 2015 haze → "100,300 excess deaths across Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore." (The "91,600 in Indonesia alone" variant is UNVERIFIED.)
- Lam et al. 2024, *Int. J. Climatol.* 44, 2087–2105. DOI 10.1002/joc.8442.
- **Lam et al. 2026, *npj Natural Hazards*, DOI 10.1038/s44304-026-00209-4 (abstract fetched): severe-fire risk boosted 2.7× by El Niño but "such risk exists regardless of the ENSO phase" (17.4% exceedance in neutral, 1.8% La Niña); IOD and extratropical Rossby wave trains can "decouple the rainfall response from the ENSO state."** Best available citation for why single-event teleconnection "failures" (e.g., muted 2023-24 MC response) are statistically unsurprising.
- Gap flagged: no dedicated 2023-24 Maritime Continent post-mortem paper located (search budget exhausted); treat as an open literature gap, not a negative finding.

## East Asia (decaying-El Niño summer signal)

- Wang, Wu & Fu 2000, *J. Climate* 13, 1517–1536. DOI 10.1175/1520-0442(2000)013<1517:PEATHD>2.0.CO;2 (Philippine Sea anomalous anticyclone).
- Xie et al. 2009, *J. Climate* 22, 730–747. DOI 10.1175/2008JCLI2544.1 ("Indian Ocean Capacitor Effect" — note actual title; "Indo-western Pacific capacitor" naming comes from later reviews, unverified this session).
- Zhang et al. 2016, *GRL* 43. DOI 10.1002/2016GL071190. 2016 Yangtze decay-summer signal much weaker than 1998 despite comparable event strength (Silk Road pattern/Ural blocking attribution) — content paraphrased from secondary text, verify full text before quoting.
- **Caution: 2020 Yangtze floods are NOT a clean decaying-El-Niño case** (weak CP event decayed fast into La Niña; primary attribution to record +IOD). Do not group 2020 with 1998/2016 without this flag; the underlying PNAS 2021 citation is unverified.
