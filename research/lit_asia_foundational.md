Prepared 2026-08-13. Scope:
- **A.** The foundational global teleconnection papers and the IPCC AR6 assessment behind the whole map.
- **B.** Indonesia / Maritime Continent: drought and fire.
- **C.** Mainland Southeast Asia (Mekong).
- **D.** East Asia: the decaying-El Niño summer signal (Yangtze).

Later, more detailed checks of the Philippines, the Mekong, southern China and eastern Australia are in `lit_missing_asiapacific.md`.

**How things were verified**
- Every DOI was checked through the Crossref API, or against the primary PDF fetched directly. Nothing was recalled from memory.
- Evidence tags:
  - **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**: read in the full text or the primary document.
  - **[ABSTRACT-ONLY]**: verbatim from the abstract; full text not read.
  - **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**: Crossref confirms the paper and its details; its content was not read in this check.
  - **[SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]**: from news, grey literature or secondary text, or not checked against a primary source.

## A. Foundational papers and the IPCC assessment

### Key papers

1. **Ropelewski & Halpert (1987).** *Mon. Wea. Rev.* 115, 1606–1626. https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1987)115%3C1606:GARSPP%3E2.0.CO;2 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
2. **Ropelewski & Halpert (1989).** *J. Climate* 2, 268–284, the warm-event composite. https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0442(1989)002%3C0268:PPAWTH%3E2.0.CO;2 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
3. **Ropelewski & Halpert (1996).** *J. Climate* 9, 1043–1059. https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0442(1996)009%3C1043:QSOPR%3E2.0.CO;2 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
4. **Mason & Goddard (2001).** *BAMS* 82, 619–638: the operational maps of probabilistic tercile shifts. https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0477(2001)082%3C0619:PPAAWE%3E2.3.CO;2 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**

6. **McPhaden, Santoso & Cai (eds.) (2020).** *El Niño Southern Oscillation in a Changing Climate*, AGU Monograph; Section 6 is "Teleconnections and Impacts". https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119548164 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
7. **Lin & Qian (2019).** *Sci. Rep.* 9, 17543: a four-phase framework for the ENSO life cycle, and how impacts differ between eastern- and central-Pacific events. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-54090-5 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**

### IPCC AR6 Working Group I (primary PDF, text extracted) **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**

- **Annex IV, AIV.2.3.2:**
  > "Amongst the best-known teleconnections are the association of El Niño events with increased precipitation in the south-western and south-eastern USA, eastern Africa, coastal Ecuador and Peru, and northern Argentina, and with decreased precipitation in eastern Australia and the Maritime Continent, southern Africa, the Amazonian basin and much of the Indian subcontinent."
- **Chapter 3, §3.7.3:** "low confidence in the role of a human-induced influence" in observed changes in ENSO amplitude, and "no clear evidence for a recent sustained shift in ENSO beyond the range of variability."
- **Chapter 4, Executive Summary and §4.5.3:**
  > "It is virtually certain that ENSO will remain the dominant mode of interannual variability in a warmer world. There is no model consensus for a systematic change in intensity of ENSO SST variability over the 21st century… However, it is very likely that ENSO rainfall variability… will increase significantly."

  Also: "It is likely that the pattern of ENSO teleconnection over the North Pacific and North America will shift eastward."
- **A caution:** the very likely increase in ENSO rainfall variability is a projection, not something already detected. The two should not be conflated.

## B. Indonesia / Maritime Continent: drought and fire

### Key papers

1. **Page et al. (2002).** *Nature* 420, 61–65. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01131 **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**
   - The 1997 Indonesian fires released 0.81–2.57 Gt of carbon, equal to 13–40% of mean annual global fossil-fuel emissions.
   - That year had the largest annual CO₂ growth-rate anomaly since 1957.
2. **Field et al. (2016).** *PNAS* 113, 9204–9209. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1524888113
   - 2015 was the most severe fire season of the satellite era, but in longer visibility records it "ranked after 1997 and alongside 1991 and 1994".
   - The fire response is nonlinear below about 4 mm/day of rainfall on degraded peat.
   - The widely quoted "1.6–1.75 Gt CO₂-eq for 2015" comes from secondary journalism and was not checked against GFED or another primary source. [SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]
3. **Koplitz et al. (2016).** *ERL* 11, 094023. https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/11/9/094023 **[ABSTRACT-ONLY]**
   - The 2015 haze was linked to "100,300 excess deaths across Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore".
   - The variant "91,600 in Indonesia alone" is not verified. [SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]
4. **Lam et al. (2024).** *Int. J. Climatol.* 44, 2087–2105. https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.8442 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
5. **Lam et al. (2026).** *npj Natural Hazards*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s44304-026-00209-4 **[ABSTRACT-ONLY]**
   - El Niño raises the risk of a severe fire season 2.7-fold, but "such risk exists regardless of the ENSO phase": 17.4% exceedance in neutral years and 1.8% in La Niña years.
   - The IOD and extratropical Rossby wave trains can "decouple the rainfall response from the ENSO state".
   - This is the best available citation for why a single event's teleconnection failing (for example, the muted Maritime Continent response in 2023–24) is statistically unsurprising.

### Open gap
No dedicated post-mortem paper on the Maritime Continent in 2023–24 was located, because the search budget ran out. Treat this as an open gap in the literature, not a negative finding.

## C. Mainland Southeast Asia (Mekong)

No peer-reviewed citation was verified in this check.

All the quantitative claims found are grey literature or news (ReliefWeb, Save the Children, Vietnam News):
- Vietnam's "worst drought in about 90 years" in 2015–16;
- 52 of 63 provinces affected;
- about 140,000 ha salinized;
- the 2023–24 Ca Mau figures.

They are indicative only and need Mekong River Commission technical reports or a hydrology-journal follow-up before being cited. [SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]

The later check in `lit_missing_asiapacific.md` found peer-reviewed support for a Mar–May drought signal in the southern basin, which is the basis for the Southern Mekong region on the map.

## D. East Asia: the decaying-El Niño summer signal

### Key papers

1. **Wang, Wu & Fu (2000).** *J. Climate* 13, 1517–1536: the Philippine Sea anomalous anticyclone. https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0442(2000)013%3C1517:PEATHD%3E2.0.CO;2 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
2. **Xie et al. (2009).** *J. Climate* 22, 730–747. https://doi.org/10.1175/2008JCLI2544.1 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
   - The actual title uses "Indian Ocean Capacitor Effect". The name "Indo-western Pacific capacitor" comes from later reviews and was not verified here.
3. **Zhang et al. (2016).** *GRL* 43. https://doi.org/10.1002/2016GL071190
   - The 2016 decay-summer Yangtze signal was much weaker than in 1998 despite a comparable event strength, attributed to the Silk Road pattern and Ural blocking.
   - Paraphrased from secondary text; check the full text before quoting. [SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]

### Caution on 2020
The 2020 Yangtze floods are **not** a clean decaying-El Niño case. A weak central-Pacific event decayed quickly into La Niña, and the primary attribution is to a record positive IOD. Do not group 2020 with 1998 and 2016 without saying so. The underlying PNAS 2021 citation was not verified.

### Timing for the 2026–27 event
- The East Asian decay-phase signal (Yangtze and mei-yu flooding) applies to **summer 2027**.
- The Maritime Continent drought and fire signal, and the mainland Southeast Asia signals, apply to the current dry season, and again in boreal summer and autumn 2027 if the event persists.
- The Indian monsoon is covered separately.
