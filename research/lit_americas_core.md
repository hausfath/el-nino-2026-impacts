# El Niño 2026–27 map: literature check for the core Americas regions

Prepared 2026-08-13. Scope: the canonical Americas teleconnections on the impacts map.
- **A.** Northeast Brazil (Nordeste): dry, Mar–May 2027.
- **B.** Amazon: drought and fire.
- **C.** Peru / Ecuador coast: flooding, Dec–Apr.
- **D.** Southeastern South America: wet, Sep–Feb.
- **E.** Central America / Caribbean: drought.
- **F.** North America, Dec–Feb.

Later, more detailed checks of some of these regions are in `lit_missing_americas.md` (northern South America, northern Mexico and the US Southwest, Hawaii, Alaska and western Canada, the Caribbean and Central America), `lit_chile_altiplano.md` (Altiplano, central Chile) and `lit_canada_nw.md` (Canada, interior Northwest).

**How things were verified**
- Every paper was checked against Crossref publisher metadata (authors, year, journal, volume, pages, DOI). Agency pages and attribution studies were fetched directly. Nothing was recalled from memory.
- Evidence tags:
  - **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**: read in the full text or on the publisher or agency page.
  - **[ABSTRACT-ONLY]**: verbatim from the Crossref-deposited abstract; full text not read.
  - **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**: Crossref confirms the paper and its details; its content was not read in this check.
  - **[SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]**: from news or other secondary reporting, or not checked against a primary source.
- The grades in brackets (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW) rate how reliable each claim or figure is, not the map's confidence tier.

## A. Northeast Brazil (Nordeste): dry, Mar–May

**Mechanism.** El Niño displaces the Atlantic ITCZ and shifts the Walker circulation, suppressing the Mar–May rainy season.

### Key papers

1. **Hastenrath & Heller (1977).** *Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc.* 103, 77–92. https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.49710343505 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
2. **Hastenrath (1990).** *J. Climate* 3, 893–904. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
3. **Hastenrath & Greischar (1993).** *J. Climate* 6, 743–758, and *J. Geophys. Res. Atmos.* 98, 5093–5102. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
4. **Marengo et al. (2013).** *J. Climate* 26, 9137–9154. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
5. **Marengo et al. (2016).** *Theor. Appl. Climatol.* 129, 1189–1200. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00704-016-1840-8 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**

### Observed strong-event outcomes
- **2015–16:** drought figures come from secondary reporting and may mix up the Nordeste and the Amazon. Take exact figures from Marengo et al. (2016) before quoting any. [MEDIUM]
- **2023–24:** a record Mar–May drought in the Nordeste was not verified. [LOW/UNVERIFIED]

### Bottom line
The mechanism and citations are solid [HIGH]. The event-specific numbers are not yet usable.

## B. Amazon: drought and fire

### Key papers

1. **Jiménez-Muñoz et al. (2016).** *Sci. Rep.* 6, 33130. https://doi.org/10.1038/srep33130 **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**
   - October 2015 land surface temperature was 1.5 °C above the October 1997 peak.
   - 13% of the rainforest was in extreme drought in Feb–Mar 2016. [HIGH]
2. **Aragão et al. (2018).** *Nat. Commun.* 9, 536. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-02771-y **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**
   - Fire incidence in 2015 was 36% above the previous 12-year mean.
   - Drought-year forest-fire CO₂ emissions were 989 ± 504 Tg/yr, against 454 ± 496 in an average year. [HIGH]
3. **Leitold et al. (2018).** *New Phytologist* 219, 959–971. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.15110 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
   - Use this paper for 2015–16 tree mortality.
4. **Espinoza et al. (2024).** *Sci. Rep.* 14, 8107. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-58782-5 **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY, full text via PMC]**
   - The Rio Negro at Manaus fell to 12.70 m on 26 Oct 2023, the lowest since 1902.
   - The October 2023 temperature anomaly was +3 °C (1981–2010 baseline).
   - South-west Amazon rainfall ran −41 mm/month, comparable to 1997–98's −42. [HIGH]

### Attribution of 2023–24
**World Weather Attribution, Amazon (24 Jan 2024)** **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**:
> "El Niño reduced the amount of precipitation in the region by about the same amount as climate change; however, the strong drying trend was almost entirely due to increased global temperatures, so the severity of the drought currently being experienced is largely driven by climate change."

Meteorological drought was made about 10 times more likely, and agricultural drought (SPEI) about 30 times. [HIGH]

### Corrections and weaker claims
- **Correction:** Phillips et al. (2009), *Science*, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1164033, is about the 2005 Atlantic-driven drought, not El Niño. Do not cite it for El Niño.
- **Lake Tefé dolphin deaths** (more than 200 deaths, 41 °C water): secondary reporting only. [MEDIUM, SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]

### Bottom line
Strong on every count. The 2023–24 severity is mainly a warming story, which is worth saying when the region is presented as an El Niño impact.

## C. Peru / Ecuador coast: flooding, Dec–Apr

### Key papers (mechanism: coastal Kelvin waves, the coastal El Niño taxonomy)

1. **Peng et al. (2019).** *Nat. Commun.* 10, 298. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
2. **Echevin et al. (2018).** *Front. Mar. Sci.* https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2018.00367 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
3. **Hu et al. (2019).** *Clim. Dyn.* 52, 7537–7552. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**

Mechanism grade: [HIGH].

### The 2017 coastal El Niño
- **Correction:** "Takahashi & Martínez 2017 (*Clim. Dyn.*)" is about the 1925 event. For 2017, use the following:
  - Takahashi et al. (2018), *BAMS* 99, S210–S211 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
  - Garreaud (2018), *Int. J. Climatol.* 38(S1), e1296–e1302, https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.5426 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
  - Rodríguez-Morata et al. (2019), *Clim. Dyn.* 52, 5605–5622 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
  - Ramírez & Briones (2017), *IJDRS* 8, 489–492. About $3.1 billion in damage and 1.1 million people affected. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
- **Yglesias-González et al. (2023).** *PLOS ONE*. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0290767 **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**
  - 169 deaths and 194,000 people displaced.
  - About $7.7 billion in reconstruction and 68,290 dengue cases.
- Damage estimates for 2017 range from $1.4 to $7.7 billion depending on what is counted. Report them as a range.

### Observed strong-event outcomes
- **1997–98:** WHO Disease Outbreak News (25 Feb 1998) **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**.
  - 2,863 cholera cases in January 1998, against 174 a year earlier, attributed to the El Niño floods.
  - Deaths and cost should be reported as a range (200–340 deaths, about $2 billion). [MEDIUM-LOW]
- **2023, Cyclone Yaku:** the first cyclone there in about 40 years, with about 85,000 homes damaged. [MEDIUM-HIGH, SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]
- **2023, dengue:** about 200,000–270,000 cases and 200–440 deaths, depending on the source. [MEDIUM]
- **2023–24, a coastal miss** (two Peruvian government primary sources) **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**:
  - IGP's coastal index (ICEN) peaked only at "weak" (+0.98 °C, Feb 2024). The late-2023 forecast gave a 77% chance of moderate or strong.
  - SENAMHI documented north-coast rainfall deficits of up to −100% over Dec 2023–Mar 2024. This followed about S/4.5 billion (about $1.2 billion) allocated for flood preparation. [MEDIUM-HIGH]
- **Unresolved:** CIIFEN's claim that 2023 was the "most intense coastal El Niño in 20 years". The page could not be reached, and the claim is probably duration-based. Not asserted.

### Bottom line
The high tier is conditional on the coastal warmth persisting. 2023–24 shows the flood risk can fail to arrive even with large preparation.

## D. Southeastern South America: wet, Sep–Feb

### Key papers (mechanism: Pacific–South American wave train, subtropical jet, low-level-jet moisture)

1. **Grimm & Tedeschi (2009).** *J. Climate* 22, 1589–1609. https://doi.org/10.1175/2008jcli2429.1 **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
2. **Tedeschi, Cavalcanti and Grimm series**, *Int. J. Climatol.*:
   - https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.4106
   - https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.3519
   - https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.4670

   **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
3. **Barros, Doyle & Camilloni (2008).** *Theor. Appl. Climatol.* 93, 19–33. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
4. **Cazes Boezio et al. (2012).** *Rev. Bras. Meteor.* 27, 377–387. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**

Citation grade: [HIGH].

### Observed strong-event outcomes
- **2015–16:** floods in Uruguay, the Paraná basin and Paraguay in late December 2015. 100,000–150,000+ people were evacuated, and it was called the "worst in 50 years". Secondary reporting is consistent. [MEDIUM, SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]
- **May 2024, Rio Grande do Sul floods** (about 600,000 displaced, more than 180 dead). These came in the decay phase, outside the map window.
  - **Clarke et al.**, *npj Natural Hazards* 3, art. 1, https://doi.org/10.1038/s44304-025-00162-8 **[ABSTRACT-ONLY]**: warming made the event "approximately 2 (0.06–4200) times as likely", and "the recent El Niño event also approximately doubled (0.7–37 times) the likelihood."
  - The two multipliers are comparable and both have enormous uncertainty ranges; flag this whenever citing. [HIGH on the numbers]

## E. Central America / Caribbean: drought

### Key papers (the developing-year midsummer drought, or canícula)

1. **Magaña, Amador & Medina (1999).** *J. Climate* 12, 1577–1588. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
2. **Karnauskas et al. (2013).** *Atmósfera* 26, 261–281. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
3. **Maldonado et al. (2016).** *Adv. Geosci.* 42, 35–50: the ENSO connection. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
4. **Anderson et al. (2019).** *ERL* 14, 124016. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**

Citation grade: [HIGH].

### Observed strong-event outcomes
- **2015–16:** FAO, FEWS NET and ACAPS reported:
  - more than 3.5 million food insecure across Central America;
  - in the Dry Corridor, about 500,000 severely and about 1.3 million moderately affected;
  - "the worst drought in decades".

  [MEDIUM-HIGH]
- **2023–24, Panama Canal:**
  - Gatún Lake fell from 85+ ft to 79.6 ft.
  - Daily transits fell from 36–38 to 22–24, and auction slots reached $4–5 million.

  Well corroborated but secondary; the canal authority's own page could not be reached. [MEDIUM]
- **World Weather Attribution, Panama (1 May 2024)** **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**:
  - "We cannot conclude that the observed drying is attributable to human-caused climate change."
  - About a 5%-per-year likelihood under El Niño; growth in water demand was a compounding factor. [HIGH]
  - This is the opposite conclusion from the Amazon study in section B.

## F. North America, Dec–Feb

### Key papers

1. **Ropelewski & Halpert (1986).** *Mon. Wea. Rev.* 114, 2352–2362 (North America). https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1986)114%3C2352:NAPATP%3E2.0.CO;2 (DOI as verified for the map's region text) **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
2. **Ropelewski & Halpert (1987, 1989)** and **Halpert & Ropelewski (1992)**, *J. Climate* 5, 577–593. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
   - NOAA CPC's operational impacts page cites exactly these papers. **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**
   - Together they support a wet Gulf Coast and Southeast, a dry and warm Pacific Northwest and Ohio Valley, a deepened Aleutian Low and a warm northern tier. [HIGH]

### The 2015–16 Southern California miss (five corroborating papers)
1. **Siler et al. (2017).** *J. Climate* 30, 10067–10079. Indo-west Pacific sea surface temperatures overrode the ENSO forcing. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
2. **Zhang et al. (2018).** *J. Climate* 31, 555–574, "the Failed 2016 Winter Rains". **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
3. **Jong et al. (2016).** *ERL* 11, 054021. A nonlinear relationship, concentrated in Southern California. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**
4. **L'Heureux et al. (2017).** *BAMS* 98, 1363–1382. **[BIBLIOGRAPHIC]**

Grade: [HIGH].

### Observed 2023–24 outcomes
- **February 2024 Los Angeles storms:** 7.03 inches over 4–5 February, breaking a 1913 record; the fourth-wettest February; more than 300 landslides. News reports are consistent. [MEDIUM-HIGH, SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]
- **Winter 2023–24:** the warmest on record for the contiguous US. The southern wet signal "largely verified", but the exact NCEI percentile rankings were not checked. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Heating demand:** no citable primary source found for El Niño reducing US winter heating demand. [UNVERIFIED]

## Documented misses and surprises

1. **2015–16 Southern California:** a strong event with near-normal rain, the best-documented failure.
2. **2023–24 Peru coast:** about $1.2 billion prepositioned for floods that never came. ICEN peaked weak, and rainfall deficits reached −100%.
3. **2023–24 Amazon:** attribution finds warming, not El Niño, drove the drought's severity.
4. **2023–24 Panama Canal:** attribution finds El Niño, not warming, was primary, the opposite of the Amazon.
5. **May 2024 Rio Grande do Sul:** outside the canonical season; warming and El Niño have roughly equal multipliers with very wide error bars.
